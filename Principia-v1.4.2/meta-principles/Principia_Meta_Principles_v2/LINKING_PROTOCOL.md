# Linking Protocol for New Principles

## 1. Normalize the child Principle

Extract its subject system, driver/intervention, outcome, direction, conditions, boundary, testability, evidence type, and claim class.

## 2. Retrieve roots through four channels

- domain and topic overlap;
- structural relation overlap;
- shared boundary or failure mode;
- formal dependency or mechanism.

## 3. Build a short chain

Use two to four roots whenever possible:

```text
child Principle
    ├── domain mechanism or theorem
    ├── statistical / causal / measurement root
    └── resource, scale, transport, or socio-technical boundary
```

## 4. Select a typed relation

- `specializes`: scoped realization of the root;
- `depends_on`: requires its assumption, theorem, or constraint;
- `refines`: sharpens mechanism, scope, or quantitative form;
- `motivates`: explains why the new method or experiment is needed;
- `generalizes`: credibly extends a root beyond its prior scope;
- `analogous_to`: structurally similar, with no evidential transfer;
- `supports`: independent evidence raises support;
- `contradicts`: credible evidence conflicts within overlapping scope.

## 5. Apply inheritance gates

Do not create a link unless the child satisfies the parent conditions, uses compatible variables and scales, and does not silently strengthen epistemic type. An empirical child does not become a theorem because it depends on one.

## 6. Preserve unresolved gaps

When no sound root exists, record a coverage gap and propose a new reviewed Meta-Principle. Do not force a semantic-nearest relation.
